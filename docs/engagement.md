# Engagement writeup: sizing a prospect's Postgres workload on Neon

Written the way the work would be papered for a customer: the statement of
work up front, the answer and its evidence in the middle, and the artifacts
they keep at the end. The customer here is a synthetic email-marketing
platform: a contact and event database and a 200,000-row query log, both
generated by this repository and described in [`nastiness.md`](nastiness.md).
Every figure below is re-derived from committed evidence by
`tests/test_engagement.py`; nothing is an estimate unless it says so.

## Results

| | |
|---|---|
| **Answer** | **No recommendation.** Refusal `refusal-4e24a549455d`, class CRITERIA UNMET: no tested configuration in the permitted space met the 80 success criteria |
| **Why** | At 32 sessions, three query shapes stay above the 1% error ceiling even on the strongest permitted setup (4 CU, all eight candidate indexes): the daily report, the segment preview and the campaign last-touch |
| **Today, on accounts the agent never saw** | Times out on 97% of daily reports, 76% of email lookups, 67% of campaign funnels and 36% of account timelines at 32 sessions; meets 33 of 80 criteria |
| **Best tested setup** | 4 CU with indexes I1-I8 meets 49 of 80 (development prediction); fixes three of the four slow shapes outright and fails to qualify |
| **Search** | 2 of 20 experiments, 19.8 of 120 CU-h, $0.60 of $10 of model spend; Claude stopped on its own |
| **Held-out evaluation** | 120 of 120 runs scored, 0 rejected |
| **Workload profiled** | 140,154 query-log rows into 9 query shapes, over a 10.98 GB dataset |

The sections below are the working: what was agreed, what today looks like,
what the agent tried, why it said no, and how far to trust each number.

## Statement of work

**Objective.** The prospect's stated goal, written down before anything ran
([`config/goal.toml`](../config/goal.toml)): *hold p99 on the four shapes that
dominate time (the daily report, email lookup, campaign funnel and account
timeline) at 8 and 32 sessions, with no other shape worse than today, at the
lowest-cost tier that does it.* Today is their current setup: a single-column
index on every filtered column, at 2 CU.

**In scope.** Profiling the query log into shapes; turning the goal into
per-shape success criteria and freezing them; a bounded search over compute
size (0.25 to 4 CU) and eight candidate indexes; a held-out evaluation on
accounts the search never saw; a results document that reports losses and
refusals the same way it reports wins.

**Out of scope.** Memory settings (`work_mem` and its relatives), extended
statistics, query rewrites, autoscaling and writes. Each is excluded for a
stated reason in [ADR 0003](adr/0003-v1-space-tiers-and-bounded-indexes.md),
and two of them turn out to be where the answer points.

**Rules, enforced in Python and never asked of the model.**

- The 80 criteria, the load model and every input they depend on are frozen
  and hash-pinned before the first experiment
  ([`criteria.md`](criteria.md), sha256 `da51e19e`). A changed file fails CI
  and every benchmark job ([ADR 0019](adr/0019-criteria-frozen-and-pinned.md)).
- Criteria are per query shape with a sample floor on every tail. A mix-level
  p99 hid a 1.2 s to 4 ms win in the first spike, so it is never used
  ([`phase0-findings.md`](phase0-findings.md), section 2;
  [ADR 0018](adr/0018-tail-support-floors.md) for the floors).
- The search sees only the profiling slice. Its output, a candidate or a
  refusal, is hash-frozen before the held-out slice can be read
  ([ADR 0002](adr/0002-criteria-pinned-held-out-two-baselines.md)).
- Claude proposes; Python validates the proposal against the permitted
  space and budget, measures it, and decides
  ([ADR 0022](adr/0022-loop-model-proposes-python-decides.md)).
- A refusal is a valid output and still gets a full held-out evaluation of
  the baselines ([ADR 0011](adr/0011-two-valid-loop-outputs.md)).

## What today looks like

The held-out evaluation ran both baselines at 1, 8 and 32 sessions, warm and
after a restart with the cache evicted, 5 interleaved repetitions each, on the
600 of 2,000 accounts held out: 120 of 120 runs scored. Every failure was a
server statement timeout at 5 s; none was an error.

On those accounts today's setup meets **33 of 80** criteria and the untuned
default (primary keys only) meets **8 of 80**. At 32 sessions today fails most
of the queries the prospect cares about, so it has no supported "before"
latency to quote: the survivors' tails hold 2 to 18 observations against a
floor of 100. The results document prints those numbers with "under-sampled"
beside each, not as clean figures.

## What the agent tried

The full log is [`loop-log.md`](loop-log.md), every proposal quoted as Claude
wrote it.

1. **2 CU with all eight indexes**, the most informative single run. The
   first attempt was aborted by the harness (the load generator opened its
   measured window before every session had connected after a restart; fixed,
   then re-run). It fixed the email lookup and campaign funnel and left the
   daily report timing out at 32 sessions.
2. **4 CU with all eight indexes**, the strongest bundle the space allows,
   changing one thing so the effect of compute could be read cleanly. Claude
   predicted the segment preview would not move, and it did not.
3. **Stop.** Claude's reason, as logged: *The strongest permitted bundle (4
   CU, I1-I8) fails the error ceilings for s07, s08 and s09 by large margins
   and fails the s07 p95 ceiling at 32 sessions. s08 failures do not change
   with any index or tier change, including the PK-only default, so no
   cheaper or different permitted bundle can qualify. Refusal is the honest
   outcome.*

It stopped with 18 experiments and about 100 CU-h of budget left. It did not
keep searching to escape the refusal, and the loop never tested 4 CU minus an
index, which is why the statement says *no tested candidate* rather than *no
candidate*.

## Why it said no

Errors and timeouts at 32 sessions, warm. Today is measured on held-out
accounts; the best tested setup is the development measurement on the
profiling slice, the evidence the refusal was frozen from. The two slices
agreed on which shapes fail for both baselines
([`phase4-findings.md`](phase4-findings.md), section 3).

| Shape | Today (held-out) | 4 CU, I1-I8 (development) |
|---|---|---|
| s01 latest events per contact | 0.1% | 0.0% |
| s02 email lookup | 76.5% | 0.0% |
| s03 account timeline | 36.1% | 0.0% |
| s04 account contact list | 0.0% | 0.1% |
| s05 campaign funnel | 67.1% | 0.0% |
| s06 bounced contacts | 0.1% | 0.0% |
| s07 daily report | 96.8% | **15.2%** |
| s08 segment preview | 21.1% | **19.6%** |
| s09 campaign last-touch | 65.6% | **20.8%** |

The ceiling is 1% on every shape. Each of the three that stay over it has a
different cause, and each was named in the nastiness list before any run:

- **s08, the segment preview** (trap Q6, correlated predicates). One call in
  five pairs a region with the wrong country. The planner multiplies the two
  selectivities, expects tens of thousands of matches, and walks the primary
  key for 100 of them; the true count is zero, so it walks the whole table.
  Index I8 on `(country, region)` exists in the bundle and the planner does
  not choose it. Phase 1 measured about 16% of segment calls timing out
  against the 20% mismatch rate, and the figure barely moves across every
  configuration tested. Extended statistics would correct the estimate and
  are outside the permitted space.
- **s09, the campaign last-touch** (trap Q8, a large sort). `DISTINCT ON
  (contact_id)` over every event of a campaign; no candidate index removes
  the sort, as the nastiness list predicted. At 4 CU about one call in five
  still times out. The cause inside the timeout was not diagnosed.
- **s07, the daily report** (trap Q9). About 140,000 rows per call. The
  BRIN index bounds the scan, and the shape stays capacity-bound at 32
  sessions: 15% of calls time out warm, 28% after a restart, and its warm p95
  is 4,156 ms against a 2,000 ms ceiling, with measured support. 4 CU is the
  largest tier in the space.

## What the prospect hears

No configuration in the agreed space meets the agreed criteria, and here are
the three shapes that are why. That answer is the deliverable: a sizing that
claimed otherwise would fail in their first week of production on the same
three shapes.

What the next engagement would test, none of it measured here:

- Extended statistics on `contacts (country, region)` for s08, or a rewrite
  that lets the planner use I8.
- `work_mem`, or an index outside the candidate list, for s09's sort.
- A tier above 4 CU or a pre-aggregated daily rollup for s07.

Each needs the criteria re-agreed and re-frozen first, because each widens the
space the criteria were frozen against.

For context and not as a recommendation: the best tested setup is predicted
at $1.36 per million successful queries at 32 sessions, against today's
$4.44 measured on held-out. It fails to qualify, and it is labeled that way
everywhere it appears.

## How far to trust it

- **Profiling predicted held-out well for verdicts, poorly for intervals.**
  Today's baseline, predicted from the development pool and then measured on
  held-out accounts, agreed on **71 of 80** verdicts. But the held-out value
  fell inside the predicted 95% interval on only **14 of 30** latency
  criteria, because the interval does not include variation between jobs.
  Verdicts survived because most values sit far from their bounds; a
  candidate close to a bound would get a confidence the interval cannot back
  (R18). That coverage is now a tracked metric and stays red until fixed.
- **The refusal does not depend on that interval.** It rests on error
  ceilings missed by 15 to 21 points on three shapes, not on tails near a
  bound.
- **The agent recommends when the evidence allows.** The eval suite runs the
  real Claude proposer through the real loop on two modeled workloads, not
  this one. On the one built to qualify it chose 1 CU with seven indexes,
  which met 80 of 80 on that workload's held-out slice; on the one built to
  refuse it refused after two experiments
  ([`phase6-findings.md`](phase6-findings.md)).

## What it cost to run

The search and the held-out evaluation held 71.9 CU-h across six jobs, about
2.5 CU-h more in a job whose state was lost before the commit gate existed,
and $0.60 of model spend: about $8.50 in all at Neon Launch rates. The
per-engagement estimate for a 100 GB prospect is in
[`cost-estimate.md`](cost-estimate.md).

## What this does not say

- **The workload is synthetic, and it was written by the person tuning for
  it.** The defense is that every trap was committed in
  [`nastiness.md`](nastiness.md) before any experiment ran, the agent was
  scored on accounts it never saw, and a trap that did not bite (Q8 did not
  spill in Phase 1 sampling) is reported rather than dropped.
- **Compute tier and eight bounded indexes only.** No memory settings, no
  extended statistics, no autoscaling, no writes. Two of the three reasons
  for the refusal sit just outside that boundary.
- **Closed loop at fixed concurrency.** Each session sends its next query
  when the last one returns, so results mean performance at 8 or 32
  sessions, not at a sustained arrival rate. A slowing system is offered less
  load, which flatters the tail ([ADR 0012](adr/0012-closed-loop-results-at-fixed-concurrency.md)).
- **Neon's pageserver cache is invisible from the client.** Cache states are
  named for the procedure that produced them, not for what the cache held,
  and post-restart read as warm over the measured window (R5, R14).
- **One platform.** The fallback, latency on local Postgres with cost on
  Neon, was never needed: the Phase 0 noise gate passed. Every figure here is
  Neon Postgres, so there is one set of numbers, not two.
